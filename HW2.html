We will replace the empty values as follows: - In the Age column, missing values were replaced with the median. Although the median is relatively close to the mean in this dataset, it was chosen for its robustness against extreme values, such as infants only a few months old versus passengers in their 70s, sensuring that these outliers do not disproportionately bias the imputed data. In addition, based on the previous test we performed, we saw that there are no people with a Master’s degree (which implies that these are children) without an age, so it is known that all people who are missing an age are adults and therefore can be replaced with a median equal to 29. - In the Cabin column, we will replace the empty values with unknown since it is not possible to predict or estimate the cabin in which the people were. - In the Embarked column, we will replace the empty values with common since the probability of boarding the ship from it is the highest.

Clear_titanic <- titanic %>%
  mutate(Age = replace_na(Age,median(Age,na.rm = TRUE)),
         #For the Cabin column, we will first replace all empty cells with NA and then replace all NA with unknown.
         Cabin = na_if(Cabin, ""),
         Cabin = replace_na(Cabin, "Unknown"),
         #In the Embarked column, we will replace the empty values with the common value we calculated in the previous sections.
         Embarked = na_if(Embarked, ""),
         Embarked = replace_na(Embarked, "S"))
